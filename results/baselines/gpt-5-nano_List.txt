1|11: Entropy is like how messy or mixed up something is. It measures the amount of randomness in a system. A clean room has low entropy, a messy room has high entropy.
2|11: Imagine a deck of cards that is perfectly ordered. That is low entropy. When you shuffle it many times and the order becomes random, the entropy is high because there are many possible orders.
3|11: Entropy also means uncertainty. If you do not know what will happen next you have high entropy. If you can predict every step the entropy is low.
4|11: In science entropy often means energy spread. When a gas fills a room its energy is spread out more and entropy goes up.
5|11: In everyday life you can see entropy when you mix sugar in tea. The sugar dissolves and the system becomes more random, entropy increases.
6|11: Another simple view is that entropy counts how many possible states a system can be in. More possible states means higher entropy.
7|11: When you melt an ice cube the solid has lower entropy than the liquid because the water molecules can move around in more ways.
8|11: Entropy tends to increase in closed systems because there are more ways for things to be disordered than perfectly ordered.
9|11: In information terms entropy measures how surprised you would be by a message. A message with lots of different possible symbols has higher entropy.
10|11: A quick kid friendly analogy is entropy is like shelf space of toys. If you have a random pile there is high entropy; if you sort by type there is low entropy.
